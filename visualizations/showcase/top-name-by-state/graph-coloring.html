Why this is a graph-coloring problem

Coloring the state maps is non-trivial. Across , male names and female names appear as a state’s #1 name—far too many for every name to receive an easily distinguishable color. More importantly, when two names lead different states in the same year, their colors should not even be similar.

This becomes a graph-coloring problem. Each node is a name, and an edge connects two names when both appear as #1 names in different states during the same year. The goal is to give connected nodes visually distinct colors, avoiding collisions on the maps. Graph coloring is a famous and well-studied problem in mathematical graph theory. The maps and networks use male colors and female colors.
